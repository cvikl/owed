No PayPal refund without proof. Not a cent more.

Owed is a refund gate for a shop's AI support agent on PayPal. The AI still talks to the customer and asks for the refund. Code decides the amount from PayPal's records and the shop's, pays through PayPal, and sends the rest to a person.

The problem

Give an AI the refund button and it pays what it is asked.

A customer owed $5 asks for $25. A customer owed everything waits days while people check by hand. Both are the same gap: nobody reads the record before the money moves.

This happened to one of us last week. A chilled seafood box, due Saturday. The courier pushed it to Wednesday, then cancelled: chilled food does not keep. Days of messages for money that was plainly owed, while the delivery record said "cancelled" the whole time.

How it works

The AI asks. Code decides from the records. PayPal pays.

Owed keeps the refund call of PayPal's Agent Toolkit, create_refund, and turns it into a request. Eight rules in whole cents, read from PayPal's capture and refunds, the shop's order and the delivery record. The AI never sets an amount.

Limits, and what is next

A gate, not a support agent. Built in three days on the sandbox.

What this is not: a real deployment needs the shop's order records, a way to tie a conversation to a customer and an order, the shop's own limits, a durable ledger and a queue for the cases that go to staff. The demo is one process with the ledger in memory. The benchmark is our own test suite on a PayPal stand-in; the arena and the race are the live sandbox evidence. Next: a shop pilot with its real order records, and the delivery rule on PayPal's own tracker once the sandbox lets a tracker be marked cancelled.
